Shannon's subject (1951): ~1.3 bits/letter · our n-gram experiment: ≤ 1.84 · random letters: log₂ 27 ≈ 4.75.

What is this measuring?
  • Your guess counts carry the same information as the text — a perfect predictor could rebuild the sentence from them.
  • Letters you get first-try were redundant: the language had already decided them.
  • Why 4.75? The board has 27 keys (a–z + space). If every key were equally likely, pinning one down would take log₂ 27 ≈ 4.75 yes/no halvings — the ceiling a letter could carry.
  • Entropy is the surprise per letter; English runs ~1–1.3 bits against that 4.75-bit ceiling. The gap is redundancy.
  • The method is Shannon's Prediction and Entropy of Printed English (1951).

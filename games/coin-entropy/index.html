The ideas underneath

Entropy is average surprise

An outcome with probability q carries log₂(1/q) bits of surprise — a 1-in-4 event is 2 bits, a 1-in-a-million event is about 20. Entropy weighs each outcome's surprise by how often it happens: it is the surprise of an average flip.

Why the peak sits at 50/50

Uncertainty is greatest when neither side is favored, so H tops out at exactly 1 bit at 50/50 — and the curve is strikingly flat up there. A 60/40 coin still carries 0.97 bits; bias has to get extreme before predictability really kicks in.

Why anyone cares

Shannon proved entropy is the limit of compression: flips of a 90/10 coin can be stored in about 0.47 bits each on average, and no scheme can beat H. That single theorem underlies ZIP files, JPEG, and every byte that crosses the internet.
